You don't need your own boat to enjoy the water here. From downtown Corpus Christi and the Port Aransas harbor, you can hop on a dolphin-watch boat, take an evening cruise past the skyline, or join a party boat with a rod in your hand. And if you'd rather stay on dry land, there are piers, a causeway, and miles of surf to fish from.

This guide covers the main options, with prices, schedules, and departure points checked against each operator's own website. Boat-trip prices and schedules change with the season and the weather, so confirm when you book.

## First, the rules: licenses and life jackets

**Fishing licenses.** Texas Parks and Wildlife (TPWD) requires a fishing license with a saltwater endorsement to fish in the state's salt water.

- **Anyone under 17 doesn't need a license or endorsement.** That covers the kids.
- **A one-day all-water license** is the simplest option for a visitor or a one-time trip: $11 for Texas residents and $16 for non-residents. It doesn't need a separate endorsement, and you can buy consecutive days together.
- **A resident saltwater package**, the license plus saltwater endorsement, is $35 and runs through August 31.
- **ID rule:** starting August 3, 2026, everyone 17 and older has to show valid ID in person to buy a license, and online buyers have to have their ID validated. Get your license before the trip, because many party boats and piers expect you to show up with one.
- **Free Fishing Day:** TPWD's Free Fishing Day falls on the first Saturday in June. No license or endorsement is needed that day, though bag and size limits still apply.

TPWD sets the bag and size limits, and they change. Check TPWD's current saltwater regulations or the Outdoor Annual app before you keep anything.

**Life jackets for kids.** Under federal law, children under 13 must wear a Coast Guard–approved life jacket while a recreational boat is underway, unless they're below deck or in an enclosed cabin. Texas law also requires it on recreational boats under 26 feet. The Coast Guard sizes jackets by weight, and "infant" jackets are for children up to 33 pounds. Adult jackets don't work for kids, so don't count on a big one with straps cinched down.

Tour boats and party boats carry life jackets, but they may not stock infant sizes. **If you're bringing a baby or toddler, call ahead and ask.** It's often easiest to bring your own properly fitted, Coast Guard–approved infant or child jacket and put it on before you board.

## Bay cruises and dolphin tours in Corpus Christi

Downtown cruises leave from the **Peoples Street T-Head**, the long pier that sticks out into the bay by the Selena memorial. From the water you get the downtown skyline, the USS Lexington, the aquarium, and the Harbor Bridge.

**Texas Tease and Japonica (Harrison's Landing)**

- **Where:** Harrison's Landing, 108 Peoples St. T-Head.
- **Texas Tease:** a 74-foot yacht that runs evening bay cruises with live music and a full bar. Public tickets are $40 for the main deck or $50 for reserved VIP seats on the upper deck, plus tax.
- **Japonica:** a 50-foot open-air tour boat with a restroom on board. It's available for private charters of up to 32 people.
- **Dolphins:** the operator says mornings and sunset are usually the best times to see dolphins, but sightings aren't guaranteed.
- **Booking and charters:** call 361-881-8503, option 2.

The evening Texas Tease cruise is a bar-and-live-music outing that's aimed at adults. Ticket sellers list age limits for it, so check before you bring kids.

**SailingCorpusChristi (Capt. Miles Herndon)**

- **Where:** Peoples Street T-Head, slip 54.
- **What:** dolphin-watching trips on the bay, plus a three-hour sunset cruise with dinner and drinks listed at $350. Dolphin-trip prices vary, so call 361-946-9405.

## Dolphin tours and the jetty boat in Port Aransas

Port Aransas has more boat options than downtown Corpus Christi. It's about an hour's drive, or take the free ferry from Aransas Pass (see our family guide). Most boats leave from the harbor along Cotter Avenue and Tarpon Street.

**Neptune's Charters**

- **Where:** 154 W. Cotter Ave., Port Aransas. Phone (361) 749-6969.
- **Dolphin Watch Nature Tour and Shrimp Trawl:** listed from $38, about 1.5 hours, all ages. The trip includes a shrimp trawl, so kids can see what comes up in the net.
- **Other trips:** bay fishing, birding, and sunset cruises.
- **Food:** the operator invites families to bring their own snacks and drinks.

**Port Aransas Fisherman's Wharf**

- **Where:** 900 Tarpon St., Port Aransas. Phone (361) 749-5448. The marina office is open daily from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m.
- **Dolphin watch and sunset cruises:** these run on catamarans in the protected channels. Check the wharf's booking page or call for current prices.
- **Bay fishing:** a three-hour inshore trip on stable catamarans. Anyone 17 or older needs a fishing license.
- **The Jetty Boat:** this runs every day across to the North Jetty on San Jose Island, for fishing, birding, or beachcombing on an undeveloped island.
  - Round trips cost $25 for ages 13 and up and $12 for ages 6 to 12. Kids 5 and under ride free.
  - You don't need a reservation. Buy tickets at the marina building before your departure time.
  - Departures run hourly from 7 a.m. (6:30 a.m. in summer) through noon, then 2 p.m. and 4 p.m. Guaranteed return trips leave the island at 10:10 a.m., 12:10 p.m., 2:10 p.m., 4:10 p.m., and 6 p.m.
  - The island side has **no facilities of any kind**. Bring water, shade, food, and a plan for little ones.

## Party-boat fishing ("head boats")

A party boat sells fishing trips by the person. The crew provides rods, tackle, and bait, and helps beginners. It's the easiest, most affordable way to take kids fishing on a boat.

**Deep Sea Headquarters (Port Aransas)**

- **Where:** 440 W. Cotter Ave., Port Aransas. Phone 361-749-5597.
- **Daily bay fishing:** 3.5 hours with morning and afternoon trips, from $53 per person. These trips fish the calmer, protected waters of the Corpus Christi Channel, and the operator recommends them for families and beginners. The operator lists gafftop, whiting, and sand trout among the usual catches.
- **Offshore trips:** 5, 8, or 12 hours, from $105 per person. Expect bigger water and longer days.
- **Included:** bait, rod, and tackle. Bring a valid Texas saltwater fishing license for anyone 17 or older, plus cash for onboard snacks and crew tips.
- **Food rule:** no outside food, drinks, or coolers. Drinks, snacks, and sandwiches are sold on board, and drinks for adults include beer.
- **Fish cleaning:** available back at the dock for a fee, cash only.

With young kids, start with a short bay trip rather than an offshore run. You'll be on calmer water and back in a few hours, which helps if anyone gets seasick or bored.

## Private fishing charters

A private charter gets you a boat, a captain, and usually all your gear, for your group only. It's the way to go if you want to target redfish and trout in the bay or head offshore on your own schedule. Prices vary widely by boat size, trip length, and season, so get a written quote.

When choosing a charter:

- Ask about the captain's Coast Guard license. Texas also requires a **fishing guide license** from TPWD for anyone paid to take people fishing.
- Ask what's included, such as bait, tackle, ice, and fish cleaning, and whether the price includes fuel and tip.
- Tell them who's coming, especially young kids, and ask whether they carry infant and child life jackets.
- Everyone 17 and older still needs their own fishing license.

## Piers and shore fishing

**Bob Hall Pier (Padre Balli Park, North Padre Island)**

- **Where:** 15820 Park Road 22, inside Nueces County's Padre Balli Park. County phone (361) 949-8121.
- **What:** a long fishing pier over the Gulf. The old pier was badly damaged by Hurricane Hanna in 2020, and the rebuilt pier reopened in February 2026.
- **Fees:** the county started charging again in July 2026. Local news reports $3 to get on the pier, $4 per fishing rod, reduced rates for seniors, active military, and disabled veterans, and free entry for kids 12 and under. Confirm with the county before you go.

**Red Dot Pier (Flour Bluff)**

- **Where:** 11801 S. Padre Island Drive, on the south side of SPID just before the JFK Causeway, on the Humble Channel. Phone (361) 937-5347.
- **What:** a lighted bay pier, so night fishing is possible, with a bait shop that sells bait, tackle, snacks, and sandwiches. There's also a small boat and kayak launch.
- **Hours:** 6 a.m. to midnight Sunday through Thursday, and 6 a.m. to 2 a.m. Friday and Saturday.
- **Fees:** listed at $3 per person, $2 per fishing pole, $2 per crab net, and $2 to launch a boat. The pier asks you to call and confirm current fees. There are no rod rentals, so bring your own.
- **License:** a pier pass doesn't replace a fishing license.

**Cole Park Pier (Ocean Drive)**

- **Where:** Cole Park, 2030 Ocean Dr., on the bayfront.
- **Hours:** the city lists the Cole Park pier as open 24 hours a day. The rest of the park follows regular city park hours, 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily.
- **Why go:** it's close to town and right next to the park's playground, which is handy when only one parent is fishing. The city's splash pads run May through October. City parks don't allow alcohol or glass.

**JFK Causeway wade and shore fishing**

The JFK Causeway between Flour Bluff and North Padre Island crosses the Laguna Madre, and the shallow flats along it are a classic spot for wade and bank fishing. It's also a busy highway, so pull off only where it's safe and legal to park, and watch for traffic as you get in and out. The usual TPWD license, bag, and size rules apply. Small children shouldn't be in the water near the roadway.

**Surf fishing**

- **City beaches on North Padre Island:** a Corpus Christi beach parking permit ($12, good January 1 through December 31) is required in designated areas. You can buy one at Stripes and H-E-B stores and at the Padre Balli Park office.
- **Padre Island National Seashore:** the national park needs its own entrance pass, and the city permit isn't valid there. A license is required for anglers 17 and up, and you can't buy one inside the park. Digging for bait isn't allowed, and you can't fish in designated swimming areas. See our Padre Island National Seashore guide for the details.
- **Everywhere:** keep your lines away from swimmers, and watch the surf and rip-current conditions, especially with kids along.

## Tips for boat days with kids

- **Pick the shortest, calmest trip first.** A 1.5-hour dolphin tour or a 3.5-hour bay trip is a much better first boat ride than an offshore day.
- **Mornings are usually calmer.** Wind tends to build through the day, and operators note that dolphins are often easiest to spot early or near sunset.
- **Bring sun protection and layers.** The bay can feel cool and breezy in the evening, even in warm months.
- **Ask about food rules before you pack a cooler.** Some boats welcome snacks, and others ban outside food and coolers.
- **Bring cash** for tips, fish cleaning, and onboard concessions, which are cash-only on some boats.
- **Seasickness:** if anyone in your group is prone to it, stick to the bay trips.
