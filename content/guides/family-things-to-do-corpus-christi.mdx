Corpus Christi is a good town for kids. Most of the best family outings are near the water, plenty of them cost little or nothing, and you can usually be home in time for nap. This guide pulls together picks that work with strollers, little legs, and short attention spans. Each one has the details checked against the place's own website.

Prices and hours change, sometimes by season. The tips at the end cover what to check the day you go.

## Texas State Aquarium

If you only do one paid outing in town with kids, make it the aquarium. It sits on North Beach, right across the ship channel from downtown. The aquarium calls itself the largest in Texas, with more than 300 animal species. Highlights include a shipwreck exhibit with reef sharks, dolphins at Dolphin Bay, sea turtles at Tortuga Cay, river otters, and a stingray lagoon.

**The basics**

- **Address:** 2710 N. Shoreline Blvd. The main entrance is at the corner of E. Surfside Blvd. and Pearl St.
- **Hours:** 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily. Holiday hours vary. For Thanksgiving week 2026, it closes at 2 p.m. Wednesday, Nov. 25, is closed on Thanksgiving Day, and returns to regular hours Friday.
- **Admission (online daily tickets):** ages 13 and older $49.95, ages 3 to 12 $36.95, ages 2 and under free, military $44.95. Online tickets are good for 60 days after purchase.
- **Parking:** sold separately. The aquarium lists it at $7 per vehicle. Members park free.
- **Cashless:** admission, food, and the gift shop are card or mobile pay only.
- **Re-entry:** tickets are good all day. Get your hand stamped at the information desk if you step out.

**What's included.** Admission covers the animal presentations, the exhibits, Ocean Odyssey, and, in season, the H-E-B Splash Park. The aquarium posts a daily presentation schedule (dolphins, sharks, stingrays, turtles, otters), so check it when you arrive and plan your loop around the shows your kids care about. The aquarium says the average visit runs about four hours.

**Ocean Odyssey** is the newer indoor children's discovery center: multi-level, climbable, and themed on the aquarium's wildlife rescue work. It's open daily from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. It has toddler-friendly areas as well as bigger climbing areas, with seating nearby for parents.

**The H-E-B Splash Park** is a 15,000-square-foot Caribbean-themed splash area with slides, a big dump bucket, and a separate zone for toddlers. It's seasonal and weather-dependent, and it's closed for the season as of mid-November 2026. The aquarium recommends water shoes, and the water shuts off 15 minutes before closing.

**With a baby or toddler**

- **Strollers** are welcome. If you'd rather not haul yours from the car, the aquarium rents a limited number on a first-come basis: $6 for a single, $8 for a double.
- **Family restroom:** there's an accessible one on the first floor in the Gulf of Mexico exhibit.
- **Food:** outside food isn't allowed in the building. There is lawn seating outside for a packed lunch, and your hand stamp gets you back in. Inside, there are several food spots. Pepsi Shoreline Grill (burgers, pizza, bowls, chicken tenders) is open daily, and some outdoor stands are seasonal.
- **Sensory support:** the aquarium is KultureCity certified. Free sensory bags with noise-canceling headphones and fidget tools can be checked out at Guest Services.
- **Pets** aren't allowed. Service animals are.

**Ways to save.** A membership covers 12 months of admission and free parking, and the aquarium says it pays for itself in about two visits. Military and veterans get $5 off with ID. Members of other AZA zoos and aquariums can get up to two tickets at 50% off on-site. During select **Lone Star Days**, Texas Lone Star Card holders from Nueces, Kleberg, San Patricio, and Aransas counties pay $3 per person. Buy tickets only from the aquarium's own site, because it warns about fake discount ticket offers on social media.

## The USS Lexington, next door

The aircraft carrier museum is a short walk down North Beach from the aquarium. It works best for kids old enough to enjoy planes and climbing around a real ship. The hangar deck, flight deck, and 3D theater are the easy, stroller-friendly parts. The decks below rely on steep ladders. See our USS Lexington guide for hours, prices, and tips. Doing both in one day is a lot for little kids, so most families will want to split them up.

## Corpus Christi Museum of Science and History

This is a solid indoor pick for a hot or rainy day. The museum sits under the Harbor Bridge at 1900 N. Chaparral St., on the downtown side, and parking is free in the lot out front and across the street.

- **Hours:** closed Monday. Tuesday through Saturday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., Sunday noon to 5 p.m. From June 1 to July 31, it stays open until 7 p.m. on Fridays and Saturdays and opens at 10 a.m. on Sundays.
- **Admission (before tax):** adults and teens $15, kids 3 to 12 $11, ages 2 and under free. Seniors 60 and up, military and veterans, and teachers with ID pay $11. Members get in free.
- **Museums for All:** families with a SNAP EBT card and photo ID get reduced admission in person, $1 for kids and $3 for adults.

The museum also runs a free-with-admission summer STEAM program and occasional homeschool days.

## Cole Park and the city's free splash pads

**Cole Park** is the city's big bayfront park on Ocean Drive (2030 Ocean Dr.). It's a classic spot to let kids run while you look at the water. In 2024 the city opened a new play-for-all playground there, with swings, slides, shade, musical instruments, and a sea turtle climbing structure. It's ADA accessible and designed for ages 5 to 12. Next to it is a fenced splash pad with 23 water features, including an ADA-accessible play area. The park also has a fishing pier that's open 24 hours.

**City splash pads are free.** Corpus Christi Parks and Recreation runs five of them, at Cole Park, Bill Witt Park, Lindale Park, Salinas Park, and West Haven Park. They operate from May 1 through October, Tuesday through Sunday from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. They close Mondays for maintenance, except on holidays. The city notes that drought restrictions can change that schedule, so check the city's splash pad page before you go. As of mid-November 2026, they're closed for the season.

Regular city park hours are 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. City parks don't allow alcohol or glass containers.

## South Texas Botanical Gardens & Nature Center

This is a calmer outing on the south side, good for toddlers who like animals and walking at their own pace. It's at 8545 S. Staples St., with the entrance off S. Staples and S. Oso Parkway.

- **Hours:** 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily. Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, and Christmas Day.
- **Admission (as listed on the gardens' homepage):** adults $12, ages 60 and up, active military, and college students $10, kids 3 to 12 $7, under 3 and members free. Parking is free.
- **Free Fridays:** for November and December 2026, the gardens list free admission on Fridays through an H-E-B sponsorship.

There are resident reptiles and parrots, an iguana house, orchid and bromeliad conservatories, and a seasonal butterfly house. You'll also find a sensory garden, a kids' play area and tree house, nature trails, a wetland boardwalk, and a birding tower over a lake. Leashed, well-behaved dogs are welcome, which makes it one of the few paid attractions where the family dog can come along. The gardens' own "Plan Your Day" page encourages packing a picnic.

## Beaches with kids

For a beach day with little ones, the easiest option is **Malaquite Beach at Padre Island National Seashore**. It's a 4.5-mile stretch with no cars allowed on the sand, and it has restrooms, showers, and a big parking lot right at the visitor center. There are no lifeguards, and you need a national park entrance pass. See our Padre Island National Seashore day-trip guide for passes, safety, and summer sea turtle hatchling releases.

On the way down the Island, **Bob Hall Pier** in Nueces County's Padre Balli Park reopened in 2026 after being rebuilt. A walk out over the Gulf is a fun short stop with kids. The county charges its own admission, which is separate from any park pass.

## A Port Aransas day trip, by ferry

For kids, getting to Port Aransas the "long way" is half the fun. Drive to Aransas Pass and take the **Port Aransas ferry** across to Mustang Island. The ferries are run by the Texas Department of Transportation. They're free for cars and walk-ons and run 24 hours a day, weather permitting. The crossing takes less than 10 minutes, but the City of Port Aransas warns that waits can top an hour on peak summer and holiday weekends.

From there, Port A's beaches and small-town shops make an easy day. You can loop back to Corpus Christi down Mustang Island and over to North Padre Island instead of retracing your route. Beach parking and driving rules in Port Aransas are set by the city and differ from Corpus Christi's, so check the City of Port Aransas's current beach rules before you go.

## Local tips for families

- **Check hours the morning of.** Holiday hours, seasonal closures, and weather all change schedules, especially for splash pads and outdoor stands.
- **Mornings are best.** Heat, crowds, and nap schedules all favor an early start, especially from May through September.
- **Pack for the sun.** Outdoor play here means bringing your own shade, hats, sunscreen, and lots of water.
- **Cards over cash.** The aquarium is fully cashless. Other places vary, so carry both.
- **Memberships add up.** If you live here, an aquarium or museum membership can pay for itself quickly. A short drive and free parking make repeat visits easy.
